Why you should try Linux

Intro - Why Linux

Chances are high that if you're browsing the Linux section of my site, you are a geeky(ish) person who likes to hang around the internet following geek or tech websites, forums and youtubes. In recent years, you might have noticed a couple of things happening:

Thing the First: Windows is getting objectively worse. Complaining about Windows (and Microsoft) has been a life-long hobby for many people, except this time it isn't the usual raging nerds and algorithm-driven whinge-fest. Unlike some of the other geek pile-ons you see online, you too can feel it, every time you fire up your PC. Hell, my father who can't tell the difference between Windows and Office and Facebook complains that it's turned to garbage. You feel it when Windows shows you an advert for OneDrive and you choose "later" because "Don't remind me" isn't an option. You feel it when your brand-new cutting-edge PC or laptop, with a kings-ransom in RAM and go-fast parts feels like it's shuffling along in prison irons.

Windows has genuinely become painful to use and more importantly, it's no longer 'yours'. Microsoft has systematically removed your ability to control your PC. Sure, you can still change the wallpaper and install some software, but Windows has ceased being a product you buy and has instead simply become another vector for driving subscriptions and harvesting that sweet-sweet user-data that tech companies are so badly addicted to.

You're not alone - we all feel it.


Image: Xbox Gamepass advert within Windows

More recently I've read the response from Microsoft - they claim to have heard the criticisim and are responding (arstechnica.com) by making several changes. Except Microsoft isn't stupid - they want your data, they need your data. They will get your data. Microsoft is like an abusive-ex; they don't really change, they simply dial it back to let the heat die down, waiting for everyone drop their guard again before they resume their shitty behaviour once more except this time you're a already a little worn down so resistance is harder. Microsoft is renown for their 'second attempts' - they rarely stumble twice in a row, particularly when it comes to Windows.

Thing the Second: Can I talk to you about our Lord and Savior Linux? There has been a significant increase in articles, videos and content pumping the alternative OS as a solid solution to the Windows problem. Perhaps you're Linux-curious or have tried it in the past. As a recent Linux migrant, I want to share what I've learnt about this recent trend.

Why now is the right time

Linux has come a very long way, even in the last 5 years. Maybe you too have looked at or tried Linux in the past and perhaps it didn't work for you. I would strongly recommend revisiting it again.

Personally, I'm a 30-year Windows veteran. I cut my teeth on DOS 5.0 before building my IT career on Microsoft systems from Win95 forward which means I have a lot of momentum in one direction and a lifetime of habits. While I've dipped my toes into the Linux pond several times over the past 20-years, I could never commit to the move so it remained a novelty on secondary devices. At the time of writing, I've now been 100% Linux on my own devices for a year and I have never once looked back - so what's changed to turn this life-long MS-goon to the alternative offering?

Gaming is finally brought in from the cold

For most average tech enthusiasts, gaming is the 'killer-app' that Linux needs in order to get mainstream attention - and I admit, it was always the one thing to bring me back to Microsoft's slimy embrace. I have wanted to use Linux since I first got my hands on Ubuntu via mail-order CD and saw the promise of no more Windows licenses. However, besides dabbling with it on those secondary devices, the inability to play games always prevented me from making the switch for my main PC. The origin of this change started in 2012 with the release of Windows 8. (Odd side-note, Win8.1 was my favourite versions of Windows - despite the universally-dispised interface - I might discuss this later). This version also introduced the Microsoft Store which - like the Apple/Google App Stores - promised easy software installation for users while Microsoft took a cut of every purchase. The brains at Valve Software saw this and had a mild heart-attack. They had built their entire empire on building, and now selling games for Windows. If the Windows Store took hold, it would destroy their business. This drove Valve to develop a plan for a genuine alternative to the Windows monopoly.

Valve and Codeweavers have sunk what I imagine to be millions of dollars into developing the Proton compatibility layer. This allows many Windows games to not only work, but in many cases, as well (and sometimes better) than on Windows. Despite what I heard, I was still pleasantly surprised how well it worked. I've played around a dozen of my favourite games to date, and all of them have worked with no special effort. Install Steam, install the game and play. The only 'issue' I've found was a graphical bug in Serious Sam 2 where the Siberian WereBull would appear as a giant graphical spike in the distance before finally rendering correctly at close-range, it was so minor I didn't bother looking for a fix. More surprising was how easy it was to play classic DOS games - even easier than it was in Windows!

It's the vibe

In my own opinion, Linux of years past just looked and felt old. Sure, it was fast, but KDE 4.x and Gnome 3 were lacking the polish we saw in Windows and MacOS at the time and in terms of design and functionality, were closer to Win98 than Vista or newer. This meant that Linux Desktops always felt several years behind the curve. Additionally, and this might sound strange but the movement of the mouse just felt wrong to me and annoyed me more than I ever imagined. No amount of tweaking seemed to fix this.


Image: KDE 4 Desktop: KDE4 felt like it was stuck in the early 'naughties'.

That vibe has improved significantly in the past few years! The modern Gnome and KDE environments are both excellent, modern Desktops that provide 90% of the modern Windows/MacOS polish and experience, but even with that missing 10%, since it leaves out all the undesirable features - subscription adverts, account sign-in and endless data tracking - that makes it an easy 120% of Win11 in my book.

As for the feel of the mouse, I don't know what changed but it just feels right now. Even tweaking the settings for speed/accelleration, I can't 'break it' and make it feel like it used to so perhaps that was changed by a lower-level improvement rather than a user-setting.

I encourage anyone that hasn't tried Linux before (or recently) or maybe felt like I did and couldn't really align with Linux desktops to take a good look again because Linux is now better than ever.
