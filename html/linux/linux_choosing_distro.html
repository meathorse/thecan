Linux: Choosing a Distro

How to try Linux without committment

First thing I recommend is to try out the different Desktop Environments (DE) to find one that works for you. For most users, the Desktop Environement is the most significant choice to make when it comes to their PC - it's the 'way' the PC operates and how you go about launching programs, find your files and generally 'use' the PC. The most well-known and popular are KDE (Windows-Like) and Gnome (MacOS-Like) but most Distros offer several different DE's as options. A very popular choice for recent Windows converts is Linux Mint (linuxmint.com) which defaults to the Cinnamon DE. The great news is that you can try out all these options right now in your browser without installing anything:

DistroSea (distrosea.com) is a website that allows you to test a few-dozen different Linux Distros, and within each Distro, you get a range of DE's that are offered by that Distro. You can open each version and explore it as if it was installed on your PC to get a feel for how that DE operates. Just note that the demo's performance is slower that you would typically experience when installed on a PC.

Testing the same Distro with several DE's will give you a good feel for each DE while holding most other variables constant. Fedora (fedoraproject.org) is a good choice as it's a popular Distro and has options for the most common DEs.


Image: With DistroSea you can trial Fedora 44 in assorted Desktop Environments

What's next?

If you've found a Desktop Environment that you like, you can continue to leverage DistroSea to find the actual Distro you like.
